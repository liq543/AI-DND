# The Meridian Job

Created: 2026-09-28  ·  Ruleset: D&D 5.2 SRD (2024)  ·  Status: Complete: chapter 1 of the saga (ended Day 7, 23:43, on the mainland coast road)

## Pitch
Ocean's Eleven with real stakes. In the canal-city of Vessarine, a crew of thieves plans to rob the Meridian
Museum of Arcane Antiquities, which everyone calls unrobbable, on the night of its Lantern Gala. Each of them
lost something to the Meridian, and each will learn it took more than they knew.

## Setting
- **World / region:** The Vess Coast (map `region`). Vessarine is a lagoon city of a hundred islands, canals,
  bridges, fog and ten thousand lamps, ruled by the Gilded Council of seven merchant-princes.
- **Starting location:** the Night Market, a black-market auction in the Drowned Chapel beneath the city.
- **Tech / magic level:** gaslamp-flavoured high fantasy. Magic is regulated, and "collected" by the Meridian's
  Acquisitions Office.
- **Major powers & factions:** the Meridian (Grand Curator Lucienne Varne), the Gilded Council, the Lantern
  Watch, the Heron's Court (Valentin Crane), the Velvet Knives (rival crew). See npcs.md.

## Tone & Style
- **Genre / mood:** a stylish heist caper with noir stakes: clever plans, banter, double-crosses, real danger.
- **Pacing:** plenty of combat (the player asked for it), mixed with legwork, infiltration and social play.
  Every legwork job carries a real chance of a fight.
- **Difficulty:** standard. Death is possible but always telegraphed.

## Party Setup
- **Player character:** Kit Corvell, "the Jackdaw" (Rogue/Thief).
- **DM-run crew (full PCs):** Sera Lark (Bard/Lore). Hedda Vask and Tobias Fenwick parted ways on Day 3 (`char leave`; alive, sheets kept, now NPCs in the world). The player wants to play **evil**: build the rest of the campaign for a ruthless crew (no moral companions forced on them; recruits motivated by greed or grudges).
  They have their own opinions but defer the big decisions to Kit.
- **Level 3**, standard array, class and background equipment packages, XP advancement.
- **Dice:** player_rolls=auto (the engine rolls everything; every roll is logged and shown).

## House Rules
- SRD 5.2 as enforced by the engine. Beta: engine bugs are fixed mid-play (AGENTS.md §1.8).
- DM-run crew never become a loophole for extra loot or healing.
- Species traits the engine doesn't automate are applied through it by the DM: `--adv "Dwarven Resilience"`,
  `--adv "Brave"`, and for Halfling Luck a natural 1 on a d20 test is re-run once.

## Campaign Arc (player-facing)
Something at the Night Market is about to go very wrong, and four strangers are about to become a crew.

## How it ended
The crew double-crossed and killed the Emissary at the Old Customs House, kept the Ysmeran Eye and his 70,000 in gems, and fled Vessarine over the Saltmarsh Causeway with Nib. The saga continues in chapter two with the same crew at level 6.

## The saga (player-stated goals, 2026-10-02) — copy into every later chapter's campaign.md / secrets.md
- This campaign is **chapter one of a grand saga**: several campaigns with the same crew (Kit, Brakka, Asche, Ottilie; Nib).
- **End goal:** the crew become supremely powerful evil beings (level 20), the warlords of the most powerful evil guild in
  the realm, with a massive evil lair filled with the spoils of all their adventures (think the guild in *Overlord*). At the
  very end they defeat an incredibly powerful being and gain godlike power.
- **Along the way:** a growing commander layer (like *Pathfinder: Wrath of the Righteous*, but evil): they raise and command
  evil forces, and there is a real military campaign. Kit is a narcissistic sociopath obsessed with becoming an almighty
  god-emperor; the crew are evil and motivated by evil, but fiercely loyal to one another (to whoever Kit is loyal to).
- **Continuity:** the important events stay in the saga's memory (the chronicle); finer details stay searchable in earlier
  chapters' notes. Sheets, items and memories carry over exactly.
- **Chapter two (requested):** after a long time skip of travel, the crew takes refuge in a village far, far away. No more
  water. More travel in the chapter, with three distinct areas, one for each act. A new grand objective (not necessarily a
  heist). Deep up-front prep: robust background, secrets and contingencies, because Kit derails by nature.
