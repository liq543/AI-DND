# DM Secrets: The Hollow Crown (chapter 2 of The Jackdaw Saga)

> DM-only. Never reveal directly; let the crew discover it through play. Pick DCs before rolls; clocks move when time passes.
> Region coordinates are on the world map `hollowmark` (2 miles a square). Engine day 1 = early spring, year three of the
> Crownless War. **Midsummer is Day 93.** New moons fall on Day 7, 37, 67 and 97.

## 1. The saga's long arc (carry this section forward into every chapter)

### The myth beneath everything
- **Ysmera, the Gorgon Queen.** Before the present gods, Ysmera was the goddess of stone, stillness and the gaze, queen
  of a serpent empire whose capital was Ysm-Tharuun. Her look made things *last*: statues, tombs, the dead, the moment.
- **The War of Lids.** The young gods overthrew her. Their champion was **Oculon the Unblinking**, god of sight,
  vigilance and judgement. He tore out both her eyes:
  - **He swallowed one**, and became the Lidless god who sees everything and never sleeps.
  - **The other he could not hold**, because it was the seat of her **soul**. It was lost to mortals and passed hand to
    hand down the ages. That is **the Ysmeran Eye**.
  - **Her blind body** turned to stone in her own reflected gaze and broke along the north of the world. **The Greyspine
    is her body**: the "stone serpent" of the Hollowmark's songs.
- **What the Eye is.** It holds a god's soul, asleep. Its petrifying gaze is that soul leaking out. Whoever masters the
  Eye can draw on divinity. Whoever holds *both* eyes holds the whole of Ysmera's sight, and with it the means to unmake
  or replace a god.
- **The Lidless Court** of the Obsidian Coast is Oculon's theocracy:
  - A surveillance empire ruled by **rakshasa priest-lords, the Unblinking Choir**. Their joints bend backward and flame
    leans away from them; the Envoy was one.
  - Their mortal priests cut away their own eyelids.
  - **They want the second eye** so that Oculon can swallow it and become All-Seeing, above every other god. The Envoy's
    cover story (that he wanted to petrify a rival court) was a lie.
  - The Court cannot buy the Eye now, so it will take it.

### Where the saga goes (chapter ladder, adjustable)
- **Chapter 2 (this one), levels 6 to 10:** the crew grows from outlaws into a power in the Hollowmark. They win the
  Antler Crown or the throne, and the Lidless Court finds them.
- **Chapter 3, levels 11 to 14:** the Hollowmark at war under Kit, with the commander layer in earnest (armies,
  lieutenants, sieges). The Court sends a crusade. The guild becomes an empire's spine.
- **Chapter 4, levels 15 to 17:** conquest beyond the Hollowmark and the road to the Obsidian Coast. The lair becomes a
  citadel. Ysmera's body in the Greyspine wakes: the **Lidded Door**.
- **Chapter 5, levels 18 to 20:** apotheosis.
  - The crew faces **Oculon** (his avatar, then the god).
  - Kit takes the swallowed eye. He must decide whether to eat Ysmera's soul too, or let her rise and rule beside him (or
    behind him).
  - Godhood. The incredibly powerful being they kill at the very end is Oculon.

### Saga threads seeded in this chapter
- **Ysmera, Kit's patron (the "surprise me").** Seated in the lair and fed, her soul begins to wake, and she dreams at
  Kit.
  - **What she wants:** revenge on Oculon, her other eye back, and a body.
  - **What Kit wants:** to be the god himself.
  - **The tension:** their interests align until the very end. She is using him; he is using her. Neither knows how much
    the other knows.
  - See §4.4.
- **The Magpie's Key** is the **Key of Lids**, carved from the bone of Ysmera's eyelid. It opens what Oculon closed:
  - the Eye's reliquaries;
  - the serpent-locks of Ysm-Tharuun (Act II);
  - one day, **the Lidded Door** in the Greyspine, her tomb (chapter 4).
  Nobody in the Hollowmark knows what it is. Ysmera does.
- **Hedda Vask and Tobias Fenwick** become the saga's heroic nemeses:
  - Hedda wants justice, and her name back. Kit holds both proofs: Dane's letter, and the Curator's daybook entry that
    Thorne killed Dane.
  - Tobias wants the Eye shattered, which would cure his glass curse and free every statue.
  - See §6.4.
- **Sabine Roux**, the woman in dove-grey, is the Court's hunter in the west. If she fails she dies; if she succeeds she
  rises. She can be beaten, bought or broken, and is worth more alive than dead, to both sides.
- **Nib's future** (chapter 3 or later, not before):
  - Brakka trains him; he becomes a man and a captain of the guild.
  - The demonic gift should come from a bargain Kit makes for him, or one Nib makes himself to be worth something.
  - The best candidate: a **demon bound in the Greyspine** by Oculon's priests, which offers Nib strength for a door
    opened.
- **The lair's growth:** from the Rookery under the Grey Barrow (chapter 2), to a fortress city (chapter 3 and 4), to a
  palace of stone (chapter 5). Every chapter adds trophies to the hall of spoils.

## 2. The truth of the Hollowmark

### Deep history
- **About 3,000 years ago**, the serpent folk of Ysm-Tharuun ruled the north under Ysmera.
  - The **Grey Barrow** was their **Socket**: the shrine where the second eye was kept and where the goddess's sleep was
    tended. The Hollins Stones were its outer ring.
  - When Oculon's champions took the eye, the serpent empire died, and the forest grew over it. The Thornbound's oldest
    songs still call the stone-eyed goddess **"the Grey Lady who Waits"**.
- **About 600 years ago**, **Hollin the Horned**, the first king, made the **Thornpact** with **the Old Green**, the
  forest's ancient, hungry spirit.
  - The wood would shelter and feed his people. The king and his heirs would keep the deep wood uncut and pay it a tithe
    of blood at every coronation.
  - The **Antler Crown** (a crown of living, ever-green antler and thorn) is the pact made solid.
  - Its bearer can speak with the wood, call the **Wild Hunt** once a season, and is obeyed by the woodfolk who still
    keep the old ways.
  - It is also a chain: it roots its bearer to the realm. A crowned king who leaves the Hollowmark for more than a night
    sickens (exhaustion each dawn away), and trees grow where they bleed.
- **The Dawn** came to the Hollowmark two centuries ago. The crown kept both faiths: Dawn for the towns, the Green for the
  wood.

### The last king and the empty crown
- **King Harlan IV, "the Antler King"**, was a good, slow king. He died three years ago at Midwinter, eyes open, of a
  sleep nobody could wake him from.
  - **The truth:** Lord Chancellor **Ansel Vire** poisoned him with **the Waking Sleep**, a Lidless poison bought through
    an Obsidian agent. The victim dies with open eyes; the Lidless call it a holy death.
  - The Lidless had already chosen Vire as their man in the Hollowmark.
- **The crown vanished** the night the king died.
  - Harlan suspected Vire, and had ordered his fool, **Merriwick**, to carry the crown to the Briar Mother if he died
    strangely.
  - Merriwick slipped out of the Antler Hall with it under his motley, rode the Old Wood Road, and reached the Elderwild
    with an arrow in his back.
  - He gave the crown to the Briar Mother's wardens and died at Hunter's Rest. His grave there is marked with a fool's
    bauble on a stick.
- **The Briar Mother has the Antler Crown** in the Heartwood. She tells her own people only that "the wood holds the
  crown, and the wood will choose".
- **Prince Aldwyn**, Harlan's son by his first queen, is **alive**.
  - Four years ago, aged twenty-two, he "died" in a hunting accident: his horse came back bloody and no body was found.
  - In truth Vire's assassins caught him in the Elderwild on his way to a druid lover, **Elowen**. The Thornbound saved
    him half-dead.
  - The Briar Mother has kept him ever since in an **enchanted sleep in the roots of the Weeping Yew**: her "sleeping
    king", to be woken and crowned when she can control him.
  - Elowen, his lover, is her granddaughter. Only the Briar Mother, Elowen and two wardens know.
  - **Corvane Redthorn suspects.**
  - Aldwyn would now be about **twenty-six**. Kit is about thirty, dark-haired and grey-eyed; Aldwyn was dark-haired
    and grey-eyed too. **Kit could pass for the lost prince.** That's §3's pretender path.
- **Princess Elinor**, aged eleven, is Harlan's daughter by his second queen, and Vire's ward and puppet.
  - She saw Vire leave her father's chamber that night through the keyhole, with a little blue bottle. She has told no
    one.
  - She is terrified, clever, and writes letters to her dead brother that she hides in the chapel.

### The Crownless War (three years)
- **Year 1:**
  - Vire declared the Regency for Elinor.
  - The Thornbound rose, saying the Thornpact was broken and the wood would crown its own.
  - The Charter League declared neutrality and sold to both.
  - **Redfield** (63,57) was a three-way bloodbath in the rain.
  - The **Ashen March** (48,38): a Regency column burned in the Elderwild.
- **Year 2:**
  - The Lidless missionaries arrived with Obsidian gold. Vire allied with them openly ("the Watchful Church") and gave
    them land outside Kingsholt for the **Watchtower** (82,57).
  - The Thornbound began burning villages that cut sacred wood, including **Burned Brindle** (14,56).
  - The Marchwood's garrisons thinned and the outlaw bands grew fat.
  - **The crew arrived.**
- **Year 3 (now): stalemate.**
  - Vire plans to crown Elinor at **Midsummer (Day 93)** with a **counterfeit Antler Crown**: real antler and gold,
    forged in Gildford by the League's master goldsmith **Hendrick Lusk**, with Lidless illusion on it.
  - Speaker Cordray of the League knows; it's how the League gets its charter of independence signed.
  - The Thornbound have sworn to burn Kingsholt if a false crown is worn.
  - The Lidless want the coronation, because a crowned puppet gives them the realm.

## 3. The factions (and how Kit climbs)

Each faction has a leader, a want, its means, its weakness, and a **door** Kit can use. Kit can take any of them, play
them against each other, or build his own. The world doesn't wait: see the clocks (§5).

### 3.1 The Regency (Kingsholt and the Crownvale; Castle Gaunt)
- **Leader:** **Ansel Vire**, Lord Protector (`noble` for his own stats; guarded by `knight`s and `guard-captain`s).
  - Fifty, silver-templed, beautifully mannered.
  - A regicide who believes he is saving the realm from rot.
  - **Lawful Evil.**
- **Others:**
  - **Princess Elinor** (`commoner`), Lawful Neutral.
  - **Marshal Edmund Strake** (`warrior-veteran`), Lawful Neutral. Old, loyal to the crown, not to Vire. He commands the
    Greencloaks and the royal army: about 3,000 men, tired.
- **Wants:** to crown Elinor at Midsummer, rule through her, crush the Thornbound, and stay solvent.
- **Means:** the army, the castles (Harrowgate, Castle Gaunt, Highbarrow), the Holdfast silver, the law, and the Lidless.
- **Weakness:**
  - It is bankrupt: it owes the League 90,000 crowns.
  - The army is loyal to the crown, not to Vire.
  - The fake crown.
  - Elinor's secret.
- **Doors for Kit:**
  - **The Pretender:** Kit as Prince Aldwyn, with the true Antler Crown, Ottilie's forged proofs, and Elinor's
    "recognition" won by charm or terror. The army and the Dawn Church would turn on Vire.
  - **The Kingmaker:** expose Vire with Elinor's testimony, and rule through the girl himself.
  - **The Creditor:** buy the Regency's debt from the League, and own the Crown's purse.
- **Attitude to the crew:** none yet. Rich foreigners in the Marchwood pay war-tax to Harrowgate, or are seized.

### 3.2 The Thornbound (the Elderwild; Elderhaugh, the Heartwood)
- **Leader:** **the Briar Mother**, Ondra Wychwood (`druid`, with a staff that casts *wall of thorns* once a day and
  *awaken*).
  - Two hundred years old, small as a child, bark-skinned.
  - She speaks for the Old Green.
  - **Chaotic Neutral**, cruel when the wood is threatened.
- **Others:**
  - **Corvane Redthorn**, war-chief (`gladiator`). A scarred woodfolk captain with a mantle of thorns. **Neutral Evil.**
    He has burned more villages than any man alive and wants the crown on his own head.
  - **Elowen** (`druid`), the Briar Mother's granddaughter and Aldwyn's lover. Chaotic Good. She is the one soft heart
    in the wood.
- **Wants:** the Thornpact restored, the deep wood uncut, a king who kneels to the wood. Corvane wants a king who *is*
  the wood: himself.
- **Means:**
  - About 1,500 woodfolk fighters and guerrilla raiders.
  - Druids, awakened trees, wolves, owlbears.
  - Two **treants** at the Heartwood.
  - An uneasy truce with the **green dragon** of the Green Hollow, **Vyrmalth**, who takes a tithe of maidens and goats
    and keeps the Regency out of the east.
- **Weakness:**
  - The Briar Mother versus Corvane: a schism waiting for a knife.
  - The prophecy can be faked.
  - They can't take walled towns.
- **The prophecy** (sung at every Thornbound fire): *"When the Grey Lady who Waits opens her eye again under the hills, a
  stranger with a stone gaze will come out of the barrow, and the wood will kneel to the one the stone has chosen."*
  - It is about Ysmera's waking. Old serpent-folk lore became woodfolk song.
  - **Kit fits it**, especially if he shows the stone gaze (§4.4, the Gorgon's Mantle).
- **Doors for Kit:**
  - **The Prophet:** be acclaimed the stone-chosen. Ottilie stages it; the Mantle proves it.
  - **The Kingslayer's bargain:** help Corvane murder the Briar Mother for the crown, then betray Corvane.
  - **The Heist:** steal the crown from the Heartwood.

### 3.3 The Charter League (Gildford and the free towns of the south and east)
- **Leader:** **Speaker Ilsabet Cordray** (`noble`).
  - Sixty, a widow, iron-grey, laughs easily, never forgets a debt.
  - **Lawful Evil.**
- **Others:**
  - **Hendrick Lusk**, master goldsmith (`commoner`). Neutral. He forged the false crown.
  - **Captain Brannoc Vaux** of **the Gilded Hand**, 800 mercenaries (`gladiator`). Neutral Evil. He sells his company
    to the best payer for the season.
  - **Mistress Greta Hask**, gem-broker: she bought most of the crew's stones and keeps a ledger of the buyers.
- **Wants:** a charter of independence, and a monopoly on the Holdfast silver.
- **Means:** money, credit, mercenaries, and the arcanists of Arcanists' Row (who sold the crew their magic).
- **Weakness:**
  - Greed.
  - Hask's gem ledger, which is a trail to the crew (§5.1).
  - The forgery of the crown, which would hang them all if it were proven.
- **Doors for Kit:**
  - **The Purse:** buy the Gilded Hand.
  - **The Blackmail:** prove the forged crown.
  - **The Partner:** offer Cordray the silver mine if Kit wins.
- **Attitude to the crew:** good customers. Hask liked them, and remembers them.

### 3.4 The Watchful Church of the Unblinking (the Lidless Mission; the Watchtower; chapels in Thornbury and the towns)
- **Leader:** **Mother Sabine Roux**, the woman in dove-grey, now Missioner of the West (`spy`, with the Mission's
  resources; see npcs.md).
  - **Lawful Evil.**
  - She failed the Court once. Her life is forfeit unless she brings the Eye back by the end of this year. A
    **rakshasa of the Choir, Vashti Ul-Amar**, is coming in person to collect: either the Eye, or her.
- **Others:**
  - **Brother Ennis** of Thornbury chapel (`priest-acolyte`). Lidless (no eyelids). Lawful Evil. He's patient, kind to
    children, and runs a network of paid **Watchers** (village informers, a silver penny a week).
  - **The Unblinking Choir's agent, Vashti** (`rakshasa`, CR 13), arrives in Act III. She's an Act III catastrophe if
    met head-on: avoid, trick, or bargain. Only at full strength (level 10, prepared, with allies) should a fight be
    survivable.
- **Wants:**
  - The Eye, first.
  - Then the Hollowmark as a Lidless province through Vire and Elinor.
  - Then every soul watched.
- **Means:**
  - Obsidian gold.
  - Informers in every village (the Watchers).
  - Truth-magic (*zone of truth* and *detect thoughts*; Kit's Ring of Mind Shielding beats both).
  - A small company of **Eyeless Brothers** (`cultist-fanatic`).
  - Coming: **the Seeing Glass** (§5.1), an Oculon relic that pierces the Stillness.
- **Weakness:**
  - Foreign and hated by the Thornbound.
  - Sabine's desperation (she can be bargained with).
  - The Seeing Glass must travel overland by caravan from Ambersell. It can be intercepted.
- **Doors for Kit:**
  - Turn Sabine, by offering her a way to live that isn't the Eye.
  - Destroy the mission.
  - Feed the Court a false Eye: Asche can forge anything, given a model. The Eye's reliquary is the model.

### 3.5 The outlaws of the Marchwood (the grass roots)

  1. Take Gallows Oak (by duel, by murder, or by out-paying Mother Gallows).
  2. Fold in the Antler Wolves.
  3. Declare a fifth power.
  This is the fastest route to followers: 50 to 80 cutthroats by the end of Act I.

  - **Father Gaspard**, abbot of Saint Wendrel's (`priest`): greedy, frightened, and sold them the barrow.

## 4. The Rookery and the Stillness (final: the player approved the design on 2026-10-02)

### 4.1 The Grey Barrow (21,62) and the lair beneath, **the Rookery** (maps: `barrow-gate`, `rookery`, `socket`)
- **The site:** a long grey hill-tomb in the Barrow Hills, three miles north-west of Crowsfoot up the Barrow track.
  - Yew-dark woods, the Hollins Stones on the next hill (22,61).
  - The barrow's outer chamber is an empty tomb (a decoy).
  - Behind a sealed serpent-door that the **Magpie's Key** opened, the serpent temple goes down in three levels.
  - A year of masons, carpenters and gold has turned it into a palace.
- **Abundance** (spent 25,000 crowns; the bones of the temple were already there): this is a lair for people with
  crowns to spare.
  - **The Barrow Gate:** a forecourt of yews and the tomb door. Inside: a vestibule with a black-marble floor, a
    porter's lodge, and a stair down.
  - **The Great Hall:** a vaulted serpent-hall with roots in the ceiling. A long black-oak table for forty, a hearth big
    enough to roast an ox, Vessarine silver, tapestries bought from a ruined Crownvale castle, and a minstrels'
    gallery.
  - **The Hall of Spoils:** a trophy gallery with niches and glass cases for chapter one's trophies (the list is in the
    chronicle), and room for hundreds more.
  - **Kit's apartments:** a bedchamber with a black-oak bed hung in silk, a dressing room, a private study with a map
    table of the Hollowmark, and a secret door to the Socket stair.
  - **Ottilie's theatre:** a jewel-box salon with a little stage, costume racks, mirrors, a forger's desk with seals of
    half the realm's lords, and her dressing room.
  - **Asche's sanctum:**
    - A library of two thousand books, many bought from the abbey's lean years.
    - An alchemy and forgery workshop with a press, plates and inks.
    - A summoning circle.
    - A cold room where something is sometimes laid out.
  - **Brakka's yard:** a sand pit for fighting, an armoury of racked weapons and armour stands, a practice dummy shaped
    unmistakably like Captain Thorne, and Nib's cot in the armoury loft.
  - **The kitchens and cellars:** a range, a bread oven, smoked hams, a cellar of two hundred bottles of the Hollowmark's
    best (and a few of Vessarine's), an icehouse, and a buttery.
  - **The treasury (the Counting Room):** a vault door. Inside: strongboxes, the Emissary's casket, scales, ledgers.
  - **The servants' hall** and the household (§4.3).
  - **The stables:** in a hidden dell outside, inside the Stillness. Five riding horses, the bay draft horse and the
    cart.
  - **The Coil Stair** spirals down to the oldest level:
    - **The Quiet Gallery:** the **Twelve** stand there, the masons who built the lower works, turned to stone at the
      first kindling (§4.2). Kit has already started arranging them.
    - **The Socket:** a round chamber of green stone, carved with a hundred serpents. In a cradle of black iron the
      Ysmeran Eye sits **open, facing inward** behind a hanging screen of lead-weighted curtain. Visors hang on pegs
      at the door.

### 4.2 The Stillness (the ward)
The rules below are the player's (journal → The Stillness), except the last bullet, which the crew doesn't know.
- **What it covers:** the Grey Barrow and the land within **a mile** of it.
- **What it does:**
  - A creature not **welcomed** by the crew cannot find, see or reach the lair. Paths curl back. The barrow looks like a
    bare hill. The eyes slide off the doors.
  - Being welcomed means carrying a **grey token** (a chip of barrow stone Asche has touched to the Eye) or being led by
    the hand by a token-bearer.
  - Divination (scrying, *locate object* or *creature*, *clairvoyance* into it) shows only grey fog.
  - A creature who deliberately searches for it makes **Wisdom DC 20** to notice that something is wrong, and still
    can't find the way.
- **What it doesn't do:**
  - It doesn't fight.
  - It doesn't hide the crew once they're outside it.
  - It doesn't stop someone who is led in.
  - It doesn't stop the truly mighty: an artifact of sight (the Seeing Glass), a god's chosen, or *true seeing* from a
    caster of 6th-level spells or higher with divine sight.
- **The hunger:**
  - At each new moon a living creature must meet the Eye's gaze in the Socket and turn to stone. Its life feeds the
    veil.
  - **A missed feeding** drops the DC by 5. **Three missed** and the Stillness fails until it's fed three at once.
  - The Twelve kindled it on the new moon before Day 1. **The first feeding is due at the new moon on Day 7.**
- **Moving the Eye** collapses the Stillness for a day and a night.
- **The ward's weakness the crew doesn't know:** the Eye can't hide *from itself*. A sliver of it, which **Tobias**
  carries in his flesh, pulls toward the whole like a compass needle. Tobias can walk straight in (§6.4).

### 4.3 The household (hired abroad, paid lavishly, and afraid)

  - A foreign majordomo hired in Gildford. Unflappable, quietly murderous, and knows exactly what his employers are.
  - **Lawful Evil.**
  - He runs the house, the carts and the money, and he is skimming 2%. He thinks Kit doesn't know.
- **The cook, Mother Hobday** (`commoner`): Neutral. Deaf, and an artist.
- **Two maids and a footman:** the sisters **Ada and Bel Crisp** and **Tobin Rudge** (`commoner`). Neutral. They've never been
  to the lower levels.
- **The groom, Corlis**, a mute stableboy (`commoner`). Neutral.
- **Two house-guards:** **Harl and Bastian** (`tough`), foreign sellswords. Neutral Evil. Loyal to the pay.
- **Asche's undead** (once he uses Animate Dead): the skeletal porters of the lower stair. The servants pretend not to
  see them.
- **Wages** (SRD hirelings: skilled 2 GP a day at least, untrained 2 SP a day; the Rookery pays over the odds), **355
  crowns a month**, due at each month's end (Day 30, 60, 90), paid from Kit's purse with `coins`:
  - Quist 90; Mother Hobday 75; Harl and Bastian 75 each; Ada, Bel, Tobin and Corlis 10 each.
  - Supplies come on the carts, about 150 crowns a cart. The house runs at about **650 crowns a month** in all.
  - The supply carts come from Thornbury twice a month on the March Road. The Hanged Men have robbed them twice.

### 4.4 Ysmera wakes (Kit's patron; reveal slowly; never all at once)
- **Stage 1 (Day 1 onward): dreams.** Kit dreams of standing in a stone garden where every statue turns its head to watch
  him. A woman's voice he can't place: *"Little jackdaw. You stole me. Keep stealing."*
- **Stage 2 (after the first feeding): the voice in the Socket.** Through the Eye's open gaze, a slow cold voice speaks to
  whoever stands visored before it, but only Kit hears the words. She offers **statue-sight**:
  - Once a day, Kit may stand before the Eye and look out through the eyes of any statue, carved face or stone figure in
    the Hollowmark: the saints over the abbey door, the kings in the Antler Hall, the gargoyles of the Watchtower, the
    Hollins Stones.
  - **The cost:** each use after the first in a tenday leaves a grey fleck in Kit's eye. At three flecks he gains a level
    of exhaustion until the next feeding.
  - This is the crew's intrigue engine.
- **Stage 3 (Act II): the Gorgon's Mantle.** She tells Kit where her high priest's mantle lies in Ysm-Tharuun.
  - It's a cloak of shed serpent-skin and green-gold scales: a **Rare** wondrous item, attunement by a creature she
    chooses.
  - **Gaze:** once a day, as a Magic action, a 30-foot cone, Con save DC 15 or Restrained by creeping stone; a second
    failed save at the end of the target's next turn petrifies it. Visors and averted eyes defend as normal.
  - **Hiss:** Advantage on Intimidation checks against those who saw the gaze.
  - It's the stone-eyed sign the Thornbound prophecy waits for.
- **Stage 4 (Act III): her price.** She asks Kit for a **vessel**: a living woman of royal blood laid before the Eye, so
  she can wear the body.
  - She suggests **Princess Elinor**.
  - The player decides. Either way it's a saga-defining evil choice.
- **What she never tells him:** that she means to wear *him* in the end, if no other vessel serves.

### 5.2 The war (the seasons)

## 6. The acts

### 6.1 Act I: Square One (the Marchwood; sessions 1 to 4; level 6 to 7)
- **Opening (session 1, Day 1, morning):** the Rookery is finished.
  - The steward Quist presents the household and the accounts (the Hanged Men robbed the last cart).
  - Kit's first dream lies behind him (§4.4 stage 1).
  - The first feeding is due in six days.
  - **Three pressures arrive in the first days:**
    - The Hanged Men's demand: a cart driver comes back beaten with a message, *"road money, or the next one burns"*.
    - Harrowgate's war-tax letter (Day 3).
    - Ennis's Watchers sniffing in Crowsfoot.
- **The robbed cart (the truth behind Quist's report, session 1):**
  - **When and where:** Day 0, about four in the afternoon, on the March Road a mile east of Crowsfoot, where the road
    dips through a holloway of beeches.
  - **Who:** **Jessamy Crook** and six Hanged Men (`bandit`), red hangman's-knot cords on their sleeves. One of them is
    **Kester Brinn**, the reeve's son, nineteen, who kept his hood up and said nothing.
  - **The ambush:** they dropped a beech across the holloway to stop the cart, and came out of the banks.
  - **What happened:** they beat the carter **Amos Pettigrew** (two teeth out, ribs stove in), took the cart, the mule and
    the load, and drove it north up the Gallows track. Jessamy gave him the message herself: *"Tell whoever pays for this:
    road money. A hundred crowns, at the Gallows Oak by the full moon. Or the next one burns, and the carter with it."*
    He heard the others call her "Jess".
  - **Where it went:** Jessamy's camp at **the Withy Pond**, a mile west of Gallows Oak (about 26,50). The candles and oil
    are stacked in her tent to sell in Ashpole, the ale is drunk, the mule is in the horse line, and the cart is by the
    pond. **Jessamy kept Ottilie's midnight-blue silk** and is having it made into a cloak: she'll be wearing it when the
    crew meets her.
  - **What the Hanged Men know:** a rich foreign household on the Barrow track pays for good carts. They've never found
    the barrow (the Stillness). Jessamy wants to know who these people are before her mother does.
  - **The full moon is Day 22** (the new moons fall on Days 7, 37, 67 and 97).
  - **Pettigrew** is at the Crow and Kettle in Crowsfoot, in Mercy Fulk's back room. Harl paid two nights in advance, and
    Quist will go on paying until he can walk (about a tenday). Mercy has already asked him everything, and Harl too.
  - **Mercy reports to Brother Ennis** (§5.1): on Day 1 the barrow gentlemen came down in person, all four, armed and on
    good horses, to see the carter. Her report reaches Thornbury chapel by Day 3.
    She listened at the back-room door (the crew heard her and she fled). She heard Kit call the goods "very valuable",
    and nothing more.
    Day 1, 10:48: Kit asked who she sells their business to. She lied (gossip only) and he believed her.
    10:50: under Brakka's fist she confessed to being Ennis's Watcher (journal: Mercy's confession). Her next report
    would go with the Thornbury carrier on market day (Day 3), if she's alive and willing to send it.
    10:56: bound and gagged; Kit means to take her to the Rookery. Her scream wasn't heard across the green (Garrick missed it).
    If she never sends her market-day report, Ennis notices her silence by Day 10 (§5.1).
    12:16: locked in the Pens, bound and gagged. Nobody in Crowsfoot saw her taken (Garrick missed the riders twice).
    **Crowsfoot finds the Crow and Kettle empty by early afternoon**: blood on a table, a smashed jug, Mercy's dress
    in rags, the carter gone. The last strangers seen were the barrow gentlemen riding in at half past ten (Garrick saw
    that). Reeve Alys Brinn will come asking at the post-box by Day 2; Brother Cuthbert adds Mercy to his list.
  - **Pettigrew** was led through the Stillness by the hand and is in the servants' quarters, nursed by Bel Crisp. He
    has seen the barrow open. Without a grey token he couldn't find it again alone.
- **Act I set pieces:**
  1. **The first feeding:** who? A Watcher? A Hanged Man? A Crowsfoot nobody? The first victim chosen, taken and
     turned. Ysmera's first words (stage 2).
  2. **The moot at Gallows Oak:** Mother Gallows's court of nooses. The crew can:
     - duel her (by Hanged Men custom the challenger fights the chief at the noose-tree, to the death or a yield);
     - poison her;
     - out-pay her;
     - turn Jessamy against her.
     The prize is about 40 cutthroats.
  3. **Old Nan Thistle of Ashpole:** the hedge-witch knows the barrow's history. **Twist:** she is a **green hag**
     (`green-hag`) and the last of the Socket's keepers. She served the Grey Lady's sleep for four hundred years and has
     waited for the Eye to come home. She offers service, or tries to steal Kit's place.
  4. **The tax-party in Crowsfoot (Day 15):** six Greencloaks bullying the village. Kill them, buy them, or let the
     village bleed. Whatever the crew does sets their name in the Marchwood.
  5. **Harrowgate:** a crag keep, about 25 men, and Sir Gerold drowning in debt.
     - **Infiltration:** Ottilie as a League debt-collector.
     - **Assault:** with the Hanged Men.
     - **Purchase:** of his debt.
     A castle is the crew's first holding above ground and makes Kit **Lord of the Marchwood**.
  6. **Brother Ennis:** a patient spider in Thornbury chapel. Killing him brings Sabine early (§5.1 jumps a step). Turning
     him is hard (he's devout). Feeding him to the Eye is poetic.
- **Act I's climax:** Kit holds a castle or the Hanged Men or both, and names his faction.
- **The hook into Act II:** a captured Thornbound raider, statue-sight of the Heartwood, or Old Nan reveals that the
  Briar Mother holds the Antler Crown, that a prince sleeps under the Weeping Yew, and that the wood waits for "a stranger
  with a stone gaze".
- **XP pacing:** about 9,000 per character across the act (to level 7 at 23,000), from 3 or 4 High fights and the
  challenges above.

### 6.2 Act II: The Elderwild (sessions 5 to 8; level 7 to 9)
- **Travel:** from the Marchwood up the Abbey Way to Bracken Ford (52,44), the Regency front, then into the deep wood
  with no roads.
  - About 3 squares (6 miles) a day in deep forest at a normal pace.
  - Random encounters: Thornbound patrols, wolves, ettercaps, a green hag's bargain, an owlbear, the Ashen March's ghosts.
- **Places:**
  - **Bracken Ford:** the front. The Regency garrison and Thornbound spies under one roof.
  - **Hunter's Rest (60,41):** Merriwick's grave, marked with the fool's bauble. The trappers know he came through with
    something wrapped in his motley.
  - **Elderhaugh (46,30):** the tree-town. The Briar Mother's court, Corvane's war-camp, and Elowen.
  - **The Heartwood (55,21):** the crown in a living throne of thorn, guarded by two treants and the Briar Mother's
    wardens (`druid`s, awakened trees, dire wolves). The heist of the chapter, if they go that way.
  - **The Weeping Yew (40,17):** Prince Aldwyn asleep in its roots, guarded by Elowen and a **dryad**.
  - **Ysm-Tharuun (30,14):** the serpent city.
    - The **Gorgon's Mantle** lies in the high priest's tomb.
    - **Guardians:** a **spirit naga** (CR 8) and serpent-cursed **medusa** priestesses (`medusa`, CR 6) who never died.
    - Serpent-locks that only the Magpie's Key opens.
    - Ysmera's history on the walls.
  - **The Green Hollow (72,24):** **Vyrmalth.** She can be bargained with (abundance: the Rookery's gold and Ottilie's
    songs), or slain for her hoard.
  - **The Sump (36,30):** a swamp of drowned serpent temples and a **shambling mound**.
- **Act II's turning points:** the crew decides how they take the wood:
  - **Prophet:** the Mantle plus Ottilie's staging.
  - **Kingslayer:** with Corvane.
  - **Thief:** the Heartwood heist.
  They decide Aldwyn's fate (kill, keep as a hostage, or replace), meet Hedda and Tobias (§5.5), and come out with the
  **Antler Crown**.
- **XP pacing:** about 25,000 per character (to level 9 at 48,000).

### 6.3 Act III: The Crown (the Crownvale and Kingsholt; sessions 9 to 12; level 9 to 10)
- **Travel:** down the Old Wood Road to Highbarrow and on to Kingsholt (86,52), or the long way round through the League
  towns.
- **Kingsholt:** the Antler Hall, the High Fane, Vire's court, the League's townhouses, the Watchtower outside the south
  gate, and the Rag Gate slums.
- **The situation:**
  - Midsummer is coming (Day 93).
  - Vire's false crown lies in the Fane's treasury, under Lidless illusion.
  - Vashti of the Choir arrives on Day 90.
  - Sabine either has the Rookery's location, or has been dealt with.
  - Hedda and Tobias are with Elinor.
  - Corvane's Thornbound march on the city.
- **Kit's endgames** (the player chooses; prepare all three):
  1. **The Pretender's Coronation:** Kit walks into the Antler Hall as Prince Aldwyn with the true crown on his head and
     Ottilie's proofs.
     - The real Aldwyn is dead, or held in the Quiet Gallery as stone.
     - Marshal Strake and the army turn on Vire.
     - Elinor must "recognise" her brother: by charm, terror, or a bargain to keep her alive.
     - **Payoff:** Kit becomes king. The crown roots him to the realm, which is a weakness for chapter 3.
  2. **The Kingmaker's Coup:** expose Vire, poison him at his own coronation feast, and rule as Lord Protector through
     Elinor.
  3. **The Brigand-Lord's Siege:** Kit's army (the Hanged Men, the Antler Wolves, the Gilded Hand, Corvane's Thornbound,
     maybe Vyrmalth) takes Kingsholt by storm on coronation night while the city is drunk.
- **Set pieces:**
  - The Midsummer feast in the Antler Hall.
  - A duel or confrontation with Hedda on the Fane steps.
  - Vashti's unmasking: a rakshasa among the priests.
  - Sabine's choice (to betray the Choir, or die).
  - The Rookery under assault on the same night (a split party, if the crew allows it).
- **Chapter end:** Kit holds Kingsholt or the crown or both. The Lidless Court declares a crusade, and chapter 3 begins.
- **XP pacing:** about 16,000 per character (to level 10 at 64,000).

## 8. When Kit goes off-script (because he will)
- **He murders a faction leader early:** the faction doesn't die. It splinters, and the most dangerous lieutenant rises.
  - Gallows dies → Jessamy.
  - Vire dies → Marshal Strake, or Vashti through a new puppet.
  - The Briar Mother dies → Corvane.
- **He burns Crowsfoot:** the Marchwood learns to fear the barrow. Recruitment by terror gets easier; the Dawn and the
  heroes get a cause. The Watchers report it all.
- **He sells out to the Lidless:** they pay gold, then betray him at the handover. Lawful Evil means "to the letter", and
  the letter always has a clause.
- **He goes straight for Kingsholt in Act I:** he finds a capital on a war footing, no allies, a Lidless tower that sees
  through disguises, and Vire's spies. The city is the hardest door; send him home with a scar and a lead.
- **He ignores the Stillness's hunger:** the veil thins. Watchers and taxmen start finding the track. Ysmera sulks and
  the dreams turn to nightmares (disadvantage on the first save of each day until she's fed).
- **He wants a dragon:** Vyrmalth can be found by Act II. Make the bargain cost something dear: a crew member's favourite
  thing, a city to burn, a hundred songs.
- **He wants more crew:** the potential lieutenants (npcs.md → Potential lieutenants) are each a session's story.

## 9. The lost years, in detail (offscreen; reveal as the player asks)
- **The road.**
  - They crossed four kingdoms in eleven months: the pass of the Saltmarsh, the Iron Plains, the city of Morrowgate
    (where Ottilie played a duchess for a fortnight), and the Ambersell road.
  - They fenced gems a few at a time in Morrowgate, Ambersell and Gildford. **Greta Hask** of Gildford bought eighteen,
    and kept a ledger with the buyers' descriptions.
- **Asche's research.** In Morrowgate's library he found the *Lithic Apocrypha*, which gave him the Socket, the Barrow
  Hills and the inversion. In the lost years he also read Tobias's notes and the Curator's daybook to pieces.
- **The barrow.**
  - Bought from Father Gaspard for 3,500 crowns a year ago, when the crew reached the Hollowmark. The abbey needed money
    for its granaries.
  - The serpent-door opened to the Magpie's Key at a touch, which made Asche very quiet for a day.
  - **The Twelve:** masons and labourers hired in Gildford and Thornbury, who worked the lower levels for a year. On the
    new moon before Day 1, Asche fed all twelve to the Eye to kindle the Stillness. Their families were told they'd gone
    to the war. Kit has started arranging them in poses.
- **Brakka** taught Nib the sword every morning for two years. Nib is good, better than Brakka tells him.
- **Ottilie** built a dozen identities in the Hollowmark's towns:
  - **Mistress Agnes Marrow**, a widow of means, in Thornbury;
  - **the Dowager of Fernwick** in Gildford;
  - **Sister Imelda** of the Dawn, forged papers and all.
- **Kit** had a villa in Morrowgate for a winter and left it burning.
